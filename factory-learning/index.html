Open Factory Lab · Learning resource

Learn ISA-95 through interactive factory scenarios

How does a manufacturing plan become an operational decision when material, equipment, quality and people impose different constraints? The Open Factory Lab is a free browser-based teaching environment for exploring that question using a fictional factory and concepts from ISA-95.

What ISA-95 helps us understand

ISA-95 (IEC 62264) provides models and terminology for enterprise and manufacturing operations integration. The lab uses its activity and hierarchy concepts to relate manufacturing workflows to the physical resources, information and decisions involved. It is a learning aid, not a standards-conformance assessment. See the ISA overview of the standard.

Four manufacturing scenarios to explore

  • Material shortage: compare a planned flow with a shortage and consider alternative responses and their downstream effects.
  • Quality hold: consider how a restriction on material use affects execution and the evidence needed for release.
  • Changeover: explore the relationship between a changed production plan, operational constraints and readiness.
  • Controlled workaround: consider the limits of a proposed exception, who can authorise it and what remains unresolved.

A practical exercise in operational decision-making

Start with a selected lesson. Establish the reference flow, introduce the disruption, select a response, and review consequences and evidence. Compare the reference, operational reality and proposed change for the same factory slice. Use the material, event and authority views to discuss dependencies and the distinction between an available signal and permission to act.

For a classroom or engineering workshop, ask participants to explain the affected resources, identify who has decision authority, and name the missing evidence before proposing a change. The facilitator notes support the debrief.

What this lab is — and is not

The four lessons use authored, deterministic teaching branches and synthetic examples. They do not provide a validated physical simulation, live plant connection, process-control capability, or demonstrated learning-effectiveness result.

The public release and its verification limits are documented in the validation record and project plan. For related exercises on operational meaning, authority and evidence, see the Operational Authority Toolkit.
